Core Verdict: Deconstructing the Episode

Episode 2 demonstrates a masterclass in situational containment, shifting the focus from initial introductions to the kinetic chemistry that defines the student council room. Rather than relying on simple gag delivery, director Naoyuki Tatsuwa and series composition lead Masahiro Yokotani construct the episode around spatial friction.

High school first-year Ume Mizunoe serves as the structural anchor—a rigid straight man whose desperate desire for academic survival constantly clashes with the chaotic energy of the upperclassmen. The episode functions by manipulating pacing through sharp tonal contrasts: rapid-fire eccentric visual skits suddenly collapse into protracted beats of awkward stillness. By treating the council room not as a passive backdrop but as a claustrophobic pressure cooker, the narrative magnifies the peculiar eccentricities of Hisako Kotobuki, Arisu Terui, Tan Otori, and Komaro Michinoku. The comedic payoff lands precisely because every interaction possesses clear physical stakes and spatial progression, transforming ordinary slice-of-life tropes into an exhilarating rhythm of escalating absurdity and delightful social panic.

Sakuga & Directional Highlights

The visual execution under character designer Ryou Imamura and director Naoyuki Tatsuwa elevates the comedy far beyond conventional gag manga adaptations, leveraging expressive animation physics and calculated layouts:

  • Elastic Character Deformations & Smear Frames: The key animation actively departs from rigid model sheets during peak comedic outbursts. When characters react in shock or scramble across the room, the animators employ exaggerated squash-and-stretch techniques, fluid smears, and loose linework reminiscent of Imamura's signature dynamic touch, infusing slapstick moments with tactile kinetic weight.
  • Ekonte & Claustrophobic Spatial Blocking: Naoyuki Tatsuwa's storyboarding uses dynamic wide-angle lenses to emphasize the physical dimensions of the council chamber. By positioning Ume in deep-focus foreground framing while background characters engage in eccentric micro-actions, the scene maintains crisp spatial clarity even amid frantic motion.
  • Compositing & Lighting Shifts: Director of photography Junpei Takatsu balances the warm, high-key afternoon palette crafted by art director Hiromichi Itou with deliberate visual punchlines. Sudden drop-shadows and stark high-contrast spotlighting isolate characters during internal monologues, punctuating comic beats with sharp theatricality.

Domestic Fan Reception: Voices from Japan

On Japanese textboards and late-night social media timelines, domestic discourse centered heavily on the adaptation's striking animation density and faithful recreation of author Maro Muchi's distinct comedic sensibility. Fans noted that rather than settling for static, dialogue-heavy staging typical of school-club comedies, the production aggressively animated every minor sight gag.

「今村亮のキャラデザが動きまくってて画面の情報量が凄い。単なる日常お色気モノに逃げず、間の取り方と作画のケレン味で原作のシュールなテンポを完璧に映像化している」

This anonymous reaction highlights the prevailing domestic sentiment: domestic viewers widely appreciated how the production team prioritized bold visual expression and kinetic pacing over passive fan service. Online commentary frequently commended the balance between surreal micro-gags and punchy comedic timing, noting that the adaptation captures the unhinged charm of the original work while standing out as a genuinely ambitious piece of animation.

Cast Performance & Subtext

The auditory landscape guided by sound director Satoshi Motoyama is characterized by impeccable comedic timing and precise register modulation across the ensemble cast:

  • Shougo Sakata (Ume Mizunoe): Sakata delivers an exceptional performance as the put-upon protagonist. His work relies heavily on fluctuating breath control, shifting effortlessly from hushed, desperate mutterings to strained, high-register vocal cracks when pushed beyond his endurance.
  • Haruka Shiraishi (Hisako Kotobuki): Shiraishi navigates Hisako's paradoxical nature with finesse, swinging between an authoritative, languid drawl fit for a student council president and an impish, breathy whine whenever her authority unravels.
  • Miyu Tomita (Komaro Michinoku): Tomita leans into a feral, feline performance filled with peculiar grunts, sub-vocal mumbles, and abrupt, clipped squeaks that lend the character an untamed, feral vulnerability.
  • Hiyori Kouno (Tan Otori): Kouno excels in deploying a mischievous, teasing cadence that masks calculated impishness under a veneer of disarming cheerfulness.
  • Shizuka Ishigami (Arisu Terui): Ishigami establishes a resonant, low-timbered sternness that deliberately breaks into subtle, trembling hesitations, making Arisu's hidden softness feel organically earned.

Streaming & Forward Look

Episode 2 solidifies the visual and narrative framework of Even the Student Council Has Its Holes!, proving that studio Passione is committed to delivering a visually kinetic comedy rather than an uninspired sequence of static talking-head vignettes. By fully exploiting the expressive visual design of Ryou Imamura and the theatrical directorial eye of Naoyuki Tatsuwa, the series has already established a confident comedic identity that turns every confined student council meeting into a canvas for fluid slapstick and dynamic character interactions.

Looking ahead, the narrative demands will test how comfortably the series can introduce peripheral figures—such as Amane, Tatsuko Aoki, and Chloe Honma—without diluting the tight room dynamic established among the central five. If the production sustains this level of expressive genga, responsive sound direction, and elastic visual humor, the anime is poised to stand as one of the season's premier character-driven slapstick comedies.
