Who this is for

Pick this up if you love unhinged action concepts and relentless combat. It hits the sweet spot for fans of grindhouse gunfights, gritty sibling dynamics, and wild mechanical designs. If you miss the raw, unapologetic energy of late-night brawl manga turned into fast-paced spectacles, you will have a blast here. Just leave your realistic physics at the door and buckle in for pure adrenaline.

You need to see this one with your own eyes. Adapted from Manabu Yashiro's web manga by Polygon Pictures, Tank Chair grabbed my attention the second its premise dropped. Most modern action series try so hard to be moody or hyper-complicated. Tank Chair goes in the complete opposite direction with sheer kinetic absurdity.

It gives you a comatose hitman bound to a weaponized wheelchair who only snaps awake when someone tries to murder him. It sounds ridiculous on paper, but the execution hits hard. It carries that rowdy spirit of old-school B-action flicks that modern television rarely dares to make anymore.

Spoiler-light synopsis

The story centers on Nagi, a legendary assassin who took a bullet directly to the brain to shield his younger sister, Shizuka. The injury left him nearly vegetative and wheelchair-bound. Shizuka refuses to give up on him. She discovers the single, twisted trigger that reboots his consciousness: sheer murderous intent directed right at his face.

To wake Nagi up and search for a permanent cure, Shizuka actively hunts down the most vicious killers, mobsters, and freaks in the criminal underworld. She drags her brother right into the crosshairs of death. The moment an enemy unleashes real bloodlust, Nagi's eyes snap wide. His souped-up combat wheelchair deploys absurd weaponry. What follows is a relentless, chaotic road trip where survival depends entirely on courting constant fatal danger.

Highlights and watch points

The Wildest Combat Gimmick in Years

Action choreography lives and dies by novelty, and turning a wheelchair into a heavily armored combat tank delivers thrills immediately. Watching Nagi drift around corners, deploy heavy artillery, and deflect bullets at high speeds is pure visual fun. Polygon Pictures leans into full mechanical madness here. The fights feel heavy, fast, and constantly surprising. It takes a trope that could easily feel like a cheap joke and turns it into lethal, high-octane spectacle.

A Hilariously Twisted Sibling Bond

Underneath all the gunfire sits an oddly heartfelt yet completely unhinged sibling dynamic. Shizuka genuinely loves her brother, yet her only way to see him awake is by shoving him into mortal peril. That contrast keeps every scene lively. Their bond provides genuine warmth amidst the grime of the criminal underground. You root for them because their dedication to each other is real, even if their therapy method involves dodging rocket launchers and sniper fire.

Non-Stop Underworld Rogues Gallery

A show like this needs colorful opponents, and Tank Chair delivers a freak show of rival assassins. Each bounty brings a bizarre fighting style or custom weapon setup that forces Nagi and Shizuka to adapt on the fly. The pacing moves quickly from one chaotic brawl to the next without dragging you through endless exposition. It embraces its pulp roots proudly and lets the punches and explosions do all the talking.
