Who this is for

Pick this up if you miss the golden era of deadpan Weekly Shonen Jump gag manga. It is perfect for fans of Kochikame, Gintama, or The Disastrous Life of Saiki K. If you want a breezy buddy-cop dynamic where god-tier psychic powers are wasted on petty neighborhood drama and workplace laziness, this show belongs at the very top of your weekend binge queue.

Here is a show that feels like uncovering a hidden gem straight from a retro comedy revival. Produced by Arvo Animation and adapted from Shun Numa's hilarious manga, Super Psychic Policeman Chojo brings unabashed slapstick back into the spotlight. In an era dominated by high-stakes fantasy epics and heavy drama, a pure comedy that genuinely makes you burst out laughing is hard to come by.

The series captures that classic neighborhood police box humor with a modern, chaotic twist. It matters because it does not try to be an existential masterclass. It just wants to make your stomach hurt from laughing at complete absurdities. For anyone tired of serious plotlines, it is an instant breath of fresh air.

Spoiler-light synopsis

Welcome to Chinjuku, a chaotic entertainment district that never sleeps. Patrol duty here is already a nightmare, but the local police box holds an even bigger surprise: Meguru Chojo. He is a senior police officer endowed with telekinesis and ESP so powerful he could easily dominate the world. Instead, he uses his mind powers to slack off, cheat on office chores, and torment his coworkers.

Enter Nao Ippongi, a rookie officer with world-class judo skills and an intense sense of justice. Nao just wants to be a proud protector of the peace, but she gets saddled as Chojo's straight-faced babysitter and partner. Together, this completely dysfunctional pair patrol the neon-lit back alleys. They take on bizarre low-level delinquents, resolve absurd neighborhood disputes, and turn routine police paperwork into an accidental supernatural battlefield. It is relentless, chaotic, and relentlessly fun.

Highlights and watch points

The Ultimate Slacker Psychic Dynamic

Chojo is not a brooding hero burdened by tremendous power. He is an overgrown child with psychic abilities who just wants to avoid honest work. Watching him exploit telekinesis for the pettiest office squabbles creates non-stop hilarity. His lazy antics bounce perfectly off Nao's earnest, hot-blooded straight-man reactions. Their banter has an effortless rhythm that carries every single scene. You do not just watch for the supernatural stunts; you watch to see how quickly Nao loses her patience with her insufferable senior partner.

Pure Chinjuku Urban Nonsense

The fictional Chinjuku district is packed with weirdos, weird mascots, and hyper-specific neighborhood troublemakers. Every side character feels like someone stumbled out of a midnight variety show. The humor moves at lightning speed, packing visual gags into the corners of every frame. The animation by Arvo Animation leans hard into exaggerated character expressions and cartoonish timing. It captures the distinct manga punchlines without dragging things out. It feels like an affectionate love letter to bustling Tokyo night districts and their eccentric locals.

Everyday Stakes with God-Tier Powers

What makes the premise click so well is the massive mismatch between Chojo's overwhelming abilities and the tiny problems he solves. He could level a city block, but instead he is using telepathy to win a stupid workplace argument or levitate a forgotten bento box. By keeping the stakes grounded in everyday police antics, the show never loses its comedic bite. It never devolves into standard battle shonen territory. The focus stays right where it belongs: purely on gut-busting situational comedy.
