Who this is for

Pick this up if you love high-stakes psychological mind games like Kaiji or Kakegurui, but wish they featured cute college girls staring into the abyss of candlestick charts. It is built for anyone who enjoys dark comedy, nail-biting tension, and seeing seemingly innocent characters push their luck to the absolute limit. You do not need any real finance background to feel the sheer terror of their terrible life choices.

I need you to look past the cute character art for a second. FX Fighter Kurumi-chan is one of the most delightfully unhinged manga adaptations to hit the screen in 2026. Animated by studio Passione, it takes the cutthroat world of retail foreign exchange trading and treats it like an extreme psychological battle arena.

In Tokyo, the manga gained an intense underground following because it captures the specific dread of watching numbers tick down in real time. It matters because it refuses to romanticize quick money schemes. Instead, it turns everyday financial risk into genuine, heart-pounding horror while keeping the banter razor-sharp and wickedly funny. You will laugh, panic, and feel physically stressed on these girls' behalf.

Spoiler-light synopsis

Kurumi Fukuga carries a dark past behind her polite smile. When she was just a middle schooler, her mother lost twenty million yen in foreign exchange margin trading and took her own life. Instead of running away from the machine that broke her family, Kurumi makes an insane vow: she will dive into the exact same market and earn every single yen back.

Now a university student, she finally saves up her starting capital and launches her trading career. She quickly discovers that forex markets do not care about tragic backstories or pure determination. Alongside a colorful crew of fellow student traders who each gamble for their own messy reasons, Kurumi faces brutal leverage traps, sudden currency plunges, and the psychological torment of unrealized losses. One wrong click can wipe out everything in seconds, and watching her try to keep her sanity is pure adrenaline.

Highlights and watch points

Genuinely Terrifying Psychological Tension

Do not let the soft aesthetic fool you. The tension here hits harder than most battle shonen. The show treats a glowing computer screen and a moving price chart like a monster waiting to strike. You watch Kurumi stare at red candles dropping, freezing up as margin calls loom, paralyzed between cutting losses or praying for a rebound. The anime captures the sweaty palms, nausea, and frantic rationalizations of someone losing money faster than they can blink. It is visceral, relatable, and shockingly suspenseful.

Morbid Humor Meets Financial Reality

What makes this story click so well is its razor-sharp balance of comedy and financial despair. Kurumi and her eccentric trading circle are hilarious, but the technical finance rules are surprisingly accurate. The show explains leverage, stop-losses, and market volatility through chaotic character dynamics rather than dry lectures. Seeing cute co-eds spiral into existential dread over sudden interest rate announcements is grimly fascinating. It pokes fun at human greed and hubris without ever pulling punches when the market inevitably retaliates against careless arrogance.

Passione's Punchy Visual Contrast

Passione does an incredible job turning dry numbers into vivid, expressive melodrama. One minute the girls are sharing tea and chatting about student life, and the next, the screen warps into high-contrast expressions of pure dread. The visual gap between the bubbly everyday atmosphere and the soul-crushing despair of bad trades keeps the pacing brisk and engaging. The directing ensures that even if you have never tracked a currency pair in your life, you understand exactly how catastrophic each bad trade feels.

Where to watch legally

You can stream FX Fighter Kurumi-chan across major global platforms like Crunchyroll, Netflix, and HIDIVE depending on your region, so check your local listings and give it an honest shot. Seriously, queue up the first couple of episodes the next time you want something that keeps you sitting upright on the edge of your seat. It is tense, bizarrely educational, and completely addictive from the opening trade.

Just remember one golden rule while watching Kurumi push her luck: admire her relentless grit from the safety of your couch, but never, ever copy her financial habits in real life.
