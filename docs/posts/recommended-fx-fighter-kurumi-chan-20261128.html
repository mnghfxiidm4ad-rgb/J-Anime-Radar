Who this is for

Pick this up if you love high-stakes psychological mind games, dark comedy disguised as cute daily life, or gambling thrillers like Kaiji and Kakegurui. It is ideal for anyone who finds tension in real-world risk. If you appreciate cute art hiding gut-wrenching suspense, panic attacks over glowing monitor screens, and cautionary tales that keep your heart pounding, this is made for you.

Look, I need to hand this to you right now. Studio Passione took a manga that Japanese internet circles were sweating over and turned it into pure distilled adrenaline. On the outside, it looks like a soft slice-of-life anime about college girls hanging out. But beneath those rounded cheeks and bright smiles lies one of the most intense, stomach-churning psychological thrillers of recent years.

The subject is foreign exchange currency trading, yet it plays out like a deadly battle anime where every single pip movement can wipe out a life's savings in seconds. It matters because it strips away heroic illusions and shows raw human greed, denial, and terror in a surprisingly grounded modern setting.

Spoiler-light synopsis

When Kurumi Fukuga was just in middle school, an enormous debt of twenty million yen from foreign exchange trading drove her mother to take her own life. Instead of running far away from the market, Kurumi makes an obsessive vow: she will win back every single yen her mother lost. Now a college student, she finally saves up enough seed capital to open her own trading account and step onto the financial battlefield.

Kurumi genuinely believes she has studied enough charts and indicators to stay disciplined and conquer the market with ease. But financial trading is a ruthless monster. Surrounded by other young women chasing their own dreams and running from their own debts, Kurumi quickly discovers how fast leverage turns an innocent click into sheer psychological horror as charts crash in real time.

Highlights and watch points

Trading Floor Psychological Horror

The genius here is how it turns Japanese yen candlesticks into pure horror cinema. You do not need a business degree to feel the blood drain from your face. When red downward lines cascade during an unscheduled central bank speech, the panic Kurumi experiences is utterly visceral. Her racing heartbeat, the sweat on her mouse hand, and the frantic bargaining of an amateur trader staring down a margin call hit with terrifying realism. It creates tension out of simple numbers on screen far better than most supernatural death games manage.

Deceptive Cute Girl Aesthetic

The contrast between the soft moe art style and the brutal psychological collapse is unforgettable. These girls look like they belong in an afternoon tea comedy, yet they are calculating leverage ratios, ignoring stop-loss triggers, and spiraling into compulsive revenge trading. Watching characters you want to cheer for make classic, devastating financial mistakes creates a unique brand of dark humor and genuine dread. It refuses to glamorize reckless risk, exposing the toxic allure of trying to double down after a crushing loss.

Relatable Flaws and Brutal Truths

What makes Kurumi so compelling is her stubborn arrogance colliding head-on with cold reality. She is smart, driven, and completely convinced she is the exception to statistical probability. Watching her rationalizations crumble when a trade goes sideways is both painful and fascinating. The supporting cast adds even more chaotic energy, showing different coping mechanisms for financial despair. You will find yourself yelling at your screen, begging her to close her positions and walk away while she still can.
