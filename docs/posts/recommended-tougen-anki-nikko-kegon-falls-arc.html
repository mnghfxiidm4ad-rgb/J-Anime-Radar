Who this is for

Pick this up if you crave gritty modern supernatural brawls with high emotional stakes. If you love traditional Japanese folklore twisted into stylish urban warfare, this arc hits the sweet spot. It is tailor-made for fans of dark battle shonen who want sharp weapon combat, raw blood powers, and underdog squads facing impossible odds in moody, real-world Japanese landmarks.

Look, if you missed the buzz around Tougen Anki, picture the classic Momotaro legend turned completely inside out. Studio Hibari brings the manga's intense momentum forward into the Nikko Kegon Falls Arc, and this is where the gloves come off. We are not dealing with a simple black-and-white fairy tale here. The conflict between the Oni bloodline and the Momotaro agency gets nasty, intimate, and deeply personal.

I tell friends visiting Tokyo all the time: this series understands the pulse of weekly manga hype. It brings kinetic pacing, savage power clashes, and sharp character designs that feel alive. It matters right now because it takes standard shonen energy and injects it with pure, uncompromising grit.

Spoiler-light synopsis

The story flips Japanese folklore on its head. Long ago, the Momotaro were celebrated as heroes who vanquished the wicked Oni. In the modern era, that ancient bloodline feud still rages in the shadows. The Momotaro operate as a cold, militaristic hunting organization, while Oni descendants just fight to survive and protect their loved ones.

Our protagonist carries cursed Oni blood, thrusting him out of normal life and into a brutal academy of survivors. In the Nikko Kegon Falls Arc, the tension boils over as a high-stakes mission takes the squad straight to the scenic, mist-shrouded gorge of Nikko. Trapped on enemy turf, the young Oni must push their volatile blood abilities past the breaking point. Hostile Momotaro assassins are waiting in ambush, turning a legendary tourist site into a deadly battleground.

Highlights and watch points

Folklore Turned Upside Down

Japanese kids grow up hearing how Momotaro was the righteous hero and Oni were monstrous demons. Tougen Anki completely flips that moral axis. Here, the Momotaro are calculated, ruthless enforcers hunting people whose only crime is their lineage. Seeing the Oni fight back using their own blood as lethal, shape-shifting weapons makes every clash feel desperate. It gives standard combat an emotional core. You actively root for the outcasts trying to claim their right to exist against an oppressive, clean-cut order.

Visceral Blood-Weapon Choreography

The combat system here is all about manipulating blood into firearms, blades, and raw destructive kinetic energy. In the Nikko Kegon Falls Arc, Studio Hibari leans heavily into fluid choreography and sharp impact frames. The battles do not pause for five minutes of internal monologues. Instead, they hit fast, crack bones, and keep characters scrambling across treacherous terrain. The clash between rigid Momotaro tech and chaotic, boiling Oni blood powers creates a visual contrast that keeps you glued to the screen.

Atmospheric Real-World Setting

Setting this critical clash around Nikko's iconic Kegon Falls is an inspired choice. Taking the violence out of nondescript back alleys and dropping it into misty cliffs and thundering waterfalls elevates the tension. You can practically feel the damp air and hear the roar of the falls drowned out by gunfire and clashing steel. It gives the arc a distinct cinematic flavor. It feels like an explosive, pressure-cooker field trip where one wrong step sends you plummeting into the abyss.
