Who this is for

Anyone who has ever hidden a nerdy passion behind a professional poker face will feel seen immediately. If you love workplace comedies, secret identity antics, wholesome adult friendships, or the sheer joy of adorable mascot merch, grab this right now. It is perfect for tired working adults who want an easy, stress-free laugh that genuinely warms your chest after a brutal commute.

Look, we have all been there. You put on your sharp work suit, act like a composed adult, and pretend you do not spend your evenings obsessing over squishy pastel plushies. That is the entire beating heart behind LIDENFILMS' adaptation of Tsutomu's beloved web manga, Uncle's Obsession with Cute Things. I remember when the manga first blew up around Tokyo bookshops.

Office workers across Japan instantly recognized that specific panic of keeping private fandom separate from corporate life. It matters because it never mocks its protagonist for having soft hobbies. Instead, it turns a middle-aged man's quiet joy into something utterly celebratory, ridiculously funny, and deeply relatable in our hyper-judgmental world.

Spoiler-light synopsis

Meet Mitsutaka Oji. On paper, he is the ultimate corporate role model: forty years old, ridiculously handsome, reliable, and revered by junior staff across his department. But step inside his locked apartment, and the stoic executive persona melts away. Mitsutaka is hopelessly, completely obsessed with adorable mascot characters, specifically a squishy yellow dog named Pug Taro.

His shelves are lined with blind-box prizes, his pillows are pastel clouds, and his inner monologue squeals with pure fanboy delight. The catch? Society expects a man his age to like golf and whiskey, so he guards his secret hobby like classified state intel. Everything falls into pure chaos when his college-age nephew suddenly needs a place to crash, threatening to expose his plushie sanctuary. It is a delightfully frantic comedy about surviving everyday life while protecting what you genuinely love.

Highlights and watch points

The Agony and Ecstasy of Secret Fandom

The comedy hits hardest because Mitsutaka treats mundane otaku struggles like covert military operations. Buying a limited-edition mascot keychain at a convenience store becomes a masterclass in stealth. Pulling a gacha capsule without making eye contact with coworkers turns into a high-stakes thriller. We watch his stone-cold executive face mask inner screams of delight whenever he sees cute merchandise. It nails that specific tension between wanting to express your passion and worrying about how colleagues might judge you, spinning everyday social awkwardness into laugh-out-loud slapstick.

Adult Friendships Built on Shared Quirks

Adults in anime rarely get to stumble into genuine, awkward new friendships, but this show handles it brilliantly. Mitsutaka eventually crosses paths with other grown men who harbor their own hidden dorky obsessions. Watching stoic corporate warriors exchange secret nods over character merchandise is both hilarious and surprisingly moving. There is zero malice or mean-spirited mockery here. The narrative treats their harmless hobbies with total respect, showing that bonding over what makes you happy has no expiration date, no matter how many gray hairs you have.

Visualizing Pure Wholesome Joy

LIDENFILMS captures the sharp contrast between drab office cubicles and sugary mascot wonderlands with great comedic timing. The show switches instantly between dark, moody close-ups that make Mitsutaka look like a dramatic hardboiled detective and pastel, sparkling fantasy sequences starring Pug Taro. That visual whiplash keeps the pacing brisk and breezy. It never feels heavy or sluggish. Every episode radiates cozy, uplifting comfort, offering the visual equivalent of sinking into a warm bath after an exhausting twelve-hour shift at your desk.

Where to watch legally

You can check this gem out across major legal streaming platforms like Crunchyroll and Netflix depending on your local region's catalog. Do not let the quirky premise fool you into thinking it is just a one-joke gimmick. This show is a giant, warm hug for anyone who has ever felt pressured to outgrow the things that spark real joy in their life.

Mitsutaka proves that growing up does not mean ditching your favorite passions to fit into a mold. Give the first couple of episodes a spin tonight. I promise his desperate quest to hug Pug Taro will put a massive smile on your face.
