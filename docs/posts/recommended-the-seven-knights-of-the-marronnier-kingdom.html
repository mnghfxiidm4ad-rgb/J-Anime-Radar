Who this is for

Pick this up if you miss old-school fairy tales with actual heart and clever worldbuilding. It is perfect for fans of cozy yet politically layered fantasy, quirky sibling dynamics, and gentle slow-burn romance. If you enjoyed works like Snow White with the Red Hair or The Apothecary Diaries, this will slide right into your comfort rotation.

You know that rare feeling when a fantasy show feels like a classic storybook you stumbled across in an antique bookstore? That is exactly what this project brings to the table. Adapted from Nao Iwamoto's acclaimed manga and animated by J.C.Staff, the story drops us into an intricate medieval continent with eight interconnected realms.

Rather than chasing explosive modern power scaling, it focuses on charm, regional culture, and family bonds. I kept telling my friends in Akihabara to keep an eye on this one because it treats fantasy worldcraft with such tender respect. It matters today because genuine fairy-tale wonder is hard to find.

Spoiler-light synopsis

Deep in the center of an eight-nation continent sits the peaceful Marronnier Kingdom. Protecting this land is General Baribara, a formidable warrior mother who raised seven deeply eccentric sons. Each brother carries a defining trait, ranging from crippling drowsiness and constant hunger to bizarre animal affinity and acute sensitivity to temperature extremes.

Despite their weird quirks, they share an earnest childhood dream: rescuing a princess in the coolest way imaginable. However, peacetime diplomacy demands real work. The brothers find themselves dispatched across the borders, each sent as an envoy to one of the seven surrounding neighboring states. What starts as a series of diplomatic visits swiftly expands into a sprawling journey. Mysterious local customs, palace secrets, and unexpected romantic encounters force each brother to step up and prove what kind of knight he truly wants to become.

Highlights and watch points

Seven Distinct Brothers, Zero Clones

A lot of large cast shows get messy fast. Here, every single brother feels instantly recognizable and endearing. Their bizarre traits are not just cheap running gags either. A knight who gets sleepy easily or one who cannot stand cold weather faces genuine logistical hurdles when visiting icy northern realms or tense summits. Watching their protective instincts clash with their goofy habits makes their sibling camaraderie deeply satisfying. You will definitely pick a favorite brother within their very first shared breakfast scene.

Rich Worldbuilding and Gentle Folklore

The continental layout gives the adventure an incredible sense of scale without feeling overwhelming. Each of the seven neighboring kingdoms has its own climate, clothing traditions, culinary quirks, and political tension with Marronnier. Walking into a new border territory feels like turning a page in a richly illustrated travelogue. The environmental design leans into soft textures and grounded architecture, giving the continent a lived-in warmth that invites you to slow down and savor the journey.

Warm Romance Balanced with Political Intrigue

Do not let the soft aesthetic fool you into thinking there are no stakes. Underneath the sweet humor, the show weaves genuine court politics, fragile treaties, and subtle diplomatic tension. The romantic subplots develop naturally out of shared danger and mutual respect rather than forced misunderstandings. It captures that sweet, slightly nostalgic shoujo-fantasy atmosphere where chivalry feels heartfelt, conversations carry weight, and every small emotional victory feels thoroughly earned.

Where to watch legally

Look for the series streaming internationally on major licensed anime hubs like Crunchyroll, HIDIVE, Netflix, or Prime Video depending on your local region. If you need a welcome palate cleanser from loud, cynical spectacles or repetitive isekai setups, let this charming kingdom win you over. It is warm, funny, visually comforting, and built on a foundation of genuine affection between its characters.

Grab a hot mug of tea, wrap yourself in a warm blanket, and let the seven brothers guide you across the continent. You will definitely not regret making the trip.
