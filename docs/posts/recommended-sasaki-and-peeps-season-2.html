Who this is for

Pick this up if you are exhausted by standard fantasy tropes and want something delightfully messy. It is perfect for tired office workers who daydream about escaping the daily grind, fans of bizarre genre mashups, and anyone who loves snarky magical animal companions. If you enjoy secret identities, espionage, and sudden shifts from high fantasy kingdoms to modern supernatural turf wars, you will feel right at home here.

Let me hand you something wonderfully bizarre. Sasaki and Peeps began as a hit light novel series penned by Buncololi with lovely art by Kantoku, before SILVER LINK. brought its eccentric world to television screens. On paper, it sounds like another middle-aged salaryman fantasy. In practice, it refuses to stay in one lane.

The show constantly throws kitchen sinks of storytelling together: isekai merchant trading, modern psychic government agents, magical girls, and aristocratic diplomacy. With Season 2 lined up to double down on that multi-layered chaos, catching up on this series right now is an absolute blast. It proves anime can still be delightfully unpredictable without taking itself too seriously.

Spoiler-light synopsis

Sasaki is an overworked Tokyo corporate drone suffocating under corporate burnout. Seeking quiet comfort, he visits a pet shop and buys an unassuming Java sparrow. But this tiny bird is no ordinary pet. It is actually Peeps, a legendary sage reincarnated from another world. Peeps gifts Sasaki magic and the ability to cross dimensions freely.

Sasaki's modest plan is simple: import cheap modern Earth goods into the fantasy realm to strike it rich and retire early. Nothing stays simple, though. As soon as Sasaki starts testing his newfound magical abilities back in modern Japan, he accidentally gets drafted into a secretive government bureau fighting rogue espers and supernatural threats. Suddenly, this tired office worker is juggling medieval merchant negotiations, cutthroat kingdom politics, and urban psychic warfare. All while trying desperately to keep his day job and his weekend sanity intact.

Highlights and watch points

The Ultimate Genre Collision

Most stories pick one sandbox and play in it. This series smashes three different sandboxes together with a hammer. One minute Sasaki is learning classical sorcery and striking trade agreements with medieval lords, and the next he is dodging bullet-hell psychic attacks in downtown Tokyo. Later, magical girls and rogue agents crash the party. It sounds unhinged because it is. Yet the show balances these conflicting worlds with surprising narrative logic. Watching these wildly disparate factions collide into a giant corporate headache for Sasaki is pure entertainment.

An Unapologetically Practical Salaryman

Sasaki is not a righteous teenager driven by destiny. He is an exhausted middle-aged professional who prioritizes job security, a decent pension, and good food. When granted godlike abilities, his immediate instinct is not heroism, but clever arbitrage and debt management. His pragmatic corporate mindset shapes every deal he strikes, whether facing magical nobility or dangerous government superiors. His deadpan chemistry with Peeps—a haughty sage who demands premium cuts of meat in exchange for arcane tutoring—anchors the entire ride in sharp comedic gold.

SILVER LINK.'s Playful Execution

SILVER LINK. knows precisely what kind of ride this needs to be. The studio keeps the pacing snappy and leans hard into the visual comedy. Sasaki's weary expressions contrast brilliantly against Peeps's smug little bird hops. The action scenes hit with snappy weight when the psychic duels ignite, but the tone never loses its breezy, self-aware charm. It never feels bloated. Instead, each episode rolls along like a brisk late-night read, setting up intricate dominoes that promise even wilder chain reactions in Season 2.
