Who this is for

Target romance and romantic comedy enthusiasts who adore the classic childhood-friend reunion trope inverted with playful gender-confusion misunderstandings. If you enjoy lighthearted school life stories where an outwardly graceful, pristine heroine lets her real, rough-and-tumble side show only around the person she trusts most, this sweet and breezy title will fit right into your seasonal viewing schedule without demanding heavy emotional investment.

Produced by romance-specialist studio Project No.9 and adapted from the popular web novel turned light novel series, Oh Boy, Was I Wrong About Her delivers pure romantic comedy comfort food. The anime embraces the ever-popular dynamic of two childhood friends reuniting under drastically altered social contexts.

While contemporary school rom-coms frequently lean into extreme high-concept gimmicks or angst-ridden melodrama, this title keeps its stakes approachable, focusing squarely on the chemistry between two teenagers rediscovering their shared history. It remains an endearing watch for international fans seeking light escapism, colorful character interactions, and that timeless charm where past camaraderie collides with newly blooming romantic awareness.

Spoiler-light synopsis

High school student Hayato moves away from his rustic hometown memories, only to step into a brand-new classroom and cross paths with Haruki, a striking, refined beauty admired by all her peers for her pristine elegance.

However, Hayato quickly realizes an astonishing truth: this ladylike idol is actually the rambunctious, mud-covered childhood friend he spent his youth roughhousing with—someone he had spent years mistakenly assuming was a boy. While Haruki maintains an impeccably poised public facade to blend into her high school environment, the second she is alone with Hayato, her composed mask completely shatters. Back reverts her unvarnished, boisterous demeanor, leaving Hayato to navigate the dizzying contrast between the tomboy pal he used to wrestle with and the breathtakingly charming young woman who now sits beside him.

Highlights and watch points

The Gap-Moe Dynamic

The core engine driving the comedy and romantic tension is Haruki's delightful split persona. In front of classmates, she plays the role of the untouchable, well-mannered young lady with flawless posture and soft-spoken grace. Yet the moment she is in private quarters with Hayato, she slumps into her casual, teasing, and outspoken habits from childhood. This duality produces continuous spark, allowing viewers to relish the intimacy of a shared secret that simultaneously highlights how much Haruki values having someone who knows her authentic self.

Nostalgia Meets Modern Adolescent Chemistry

Rather than treating the childhood friendship as a distant plot device, the narrative consistently grounds its humor in their shared rural memories. The banter flows organically because Hayato and Haruki already possess years of shared shorthand and instinctive comfort, even as the biological reality of Haruki being a girl introduces flustered pauses and sudden heartbeats. Watching them recalibrate their physical and emotional boundaries provides a cozy, heartwarming viewing experience that balances classic slapstick with genuine, tender coming-of-age affection.
